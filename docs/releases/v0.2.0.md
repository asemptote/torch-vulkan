# torch-vulkan 0.2.0

The first public release: a native PyTorch device (`"vk"`) on portable Vulkan
compute, aimed at scientific workloads (float64, complex numbers, FFTs, dense
and sparse linear algebra, special functions) on integrated, discrete and
phone GPUs, without vendor libraries.

## Highlights

- **No CPU fallbacks in PyTorch's tests.** Across 36 of PyTorch's
  device-generic test files (about 160,000 tests each on lavapipe and an NVIDIA
  GPU), no op computes on the CPU or calls it to raise an error; every
  remaining failure is shared with the CPU or listed with its reason. Every
  OpInfo op runs on the GPU and matches the CPU.
- **Results that match the CPU, independent of the driver**: Cephes-style
  float32 transcendentals, a float64 math library, correctly rounded float32
  division, sqrt and rsqrt and float64 division without fused multiply-adds, exact float8 conversions, and fixes
  for driver quirks found on Intel (Windows and Mesa), NVIDIA, Arm Mali and
  lavapipe.
- **Faster kernels**: subgroup-tiled matrix products (float32 level with MKL on
  an Intel UHD 620), float16 on tensor cores through cooperative matrices
  and bfloat16 on tensor cores (155-165 TFLOP/s on an H100 NVL or L40S, about
  95% of NVIDIA's own Vulkan sample on the H100), FFTs in register-resident passes
  with the real-to-complex step fused in (within 2x of CUDA, except
  transforms of 2^16-2^20 points at up to about 2.3x, from per-call latency),
  a matrix-vector kernel, `eigh` by blocked tridiagonal reduction and divide
  and conquer (float64 at 1.5-3.4x CUDA's time, from 50-110x), `svd` by
  bidiagonalisation (0.2-0.8x: faster than CUDA in all our measurements), and
  `solve` and `qr` with their panels spread over many workgroups (`solve`
  1.4-3.4x; `qr` of a 1000x64 matrix about 4-5x, from 20-40x).
- **Training**: fused and foreach Adam, AdamW, SGD and Adagrad, and
  `torch.amp.GradScaler`, all on the GPU.
- **New coverage**: quantisation (int8 weight-only and dynamic 4-bit matrix
  products, float8 `_scaled_mm`, per-tensor affine quantised tensors, fake
  quantisation), nested tensors, `nn.MultiheadAttention`'s fast path, Lanczos
  upsampling, complex32 and bcomplex32, `segment_reduce`.

## Installing

Wheels for Windows and Linux (x86_64), Python 3.10 or newer, PyTorch 2.14:

    pip install "torch==2.14.*"
    pip install <wheel URL from the assets below>

On Android (Termux), build from source with the prebuilt kernels: extract
`torch-vulkan-spirv-v0.2.0.tar.gz` and run `tools/build-termux.sh` with that
directory.

## Tested on

Intel UHD Graphics 620 (Windows), Intel HD Graphics 530 (Mesa ANV, Linux),
NVIDIA H100 NVL and L40S (Linux), Arm Mali-G57 (Android, Termux), Mesa
lavapipe (CI, with the validation layer).

## Known limitations

`torch.compile` has no fusing backend yet (planned for 0.3.0). One device;
tensors under 2^31 elements;
per-tensor affine quantisation only. See the README's Limitations section and
[benchmarks against CUDA](https://github.com/asemptote/torch-vulkan/tree/main/benchmarks/results).
